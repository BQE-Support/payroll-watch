Methodology & scope notes

Data source

Every ticket in the Zendesk Payroll group (group_id 35523902508311) with status New, Open, Pending, or On Hold (i.e. not yet Solved). Tickets come from the support team's shared Zendesk store, which reads Zendesk's change feed every 2 minutes; a ticket's comments are re-read whenever the ticket changes, to see who commented last and how many public comments it has. This page doesn't pull anything itself: it reads that store, the shared Jira store, and the RingCX snapshot the live wallboard already takes every minute. Flags and hours are worked out in your browser, so they stay current between refreshes.

Flag definitions (what makes a ticket "flagged"/urgent)

  • No agent reply yet: the ticket has been open 4+ hours with zero public comment from anyone other than the requester.
  • Awaiting our reply: the requester's comment is the most recent public comment, and it's been 4+ hours since then.

Heavy back-and-forth (informational, not urgent by itself)

  • 10 or more public comments on the ticket, regardless of whose turn it is to reply. This does not by itself make a ticket flagged/urgent — a long thread where we just replied and are waiting on the customer isn't a dropped ball on our side, even if the history is long. It's still worth a look, so it gets its own KPI tile and a dedicated "Heavy back-and-forth, by assignee" section instead of counting toward the urgent list.

Urgency tiers

  • Needs attention now: flagged and last activity was within 48 hours.
  • Aging: flagged, last activity 2–7 days ago.
  • Long-stale backlog: flagged, last activity 7+ days ago — often a hygiene/closure issue rather than an active miss. A cluster of these are "pending" tickets where the customer replied last but the ticket never reopened — worth checking whether an automation gap is letting these silently sit.

Known caveats

  • "Hours since activity" is calendar time, not Payroll's real 6am–4pm Pacific business hours — a ticket that went quiet Friday evening will show more elapsed hours than the team was actually available to act on it.
  • Only public comments count toward the comment total and "last activity" — internal notes are excluded, matching what the requester actually sees.
  • Assignee reflects whoever is currently assigned in Zendesk at poll time, not who owned the ticket when it went stale.

With Dev Team (GAL)

  • Detected by a GAL-##### pattern in the ticket subject — BQE's convention for a ticket linked to an Engineering Jira issue. These sit in their own section, and are excluded from every other section on this page (urgency tiers, by-assignee, by-status, by-reason, Where to Focus, and the main ticket table) so a GAL ticket never double-counts — they may be blocked on Dev, not on Support, so being on the list isn't automatically a dropped ball the way the other flags are.
  • A GAL ticket can still carry an urgency flag internally (e.g. the customer replied and we haven't followed up) — that still shows as a chip within the GAL section itself, it just isn't reflected in the KPI counts or charts for the non-GAL sections.
  • Every GAL-##### and ES-##### (Escalations) reference found in a ticket's subject renders as a clickable chip linking straight to that Jira issue on bqetrack.atlassian.net — wherever the ticket shows up on this page, GAL section or not (a ticket can carry more than one, e.g. a GAL plus one or two ES refs). Only GAL-##### still drives section membership/exclusion above; an ES-only reference doesn't move a ticket out of the normal flow.

Gusto Incidents

  • A ticket qualifies when the requester's email domain is @gusto.com and the Zendesk ticket type is "Incident" — both conditions matter, a non-incident ticket from Gusto stays in the normal flow. Gusto opens these to flag something on their end; they don't always need or get a public reply the way a real customer does (often resolved via an internal note or a call instead), so they're excluded from the reply-based flags rather than counting as a dropped ball.
  • Same exclusion treatment as GAL tickets — own section, excluded everywhere else on the page.

ES Escalations (Payroll)

  • A cross-check slice of Jira's own Escalations (ES) project: open tickets (statusCategory ≠ Done — empirically the same population as "status ≠ Closed" for this project as of this build, though the two aren't guaranteed identical in theory) whose components field contains Payroll or Reports - Payroll. This is the exact same criteria as Jira's own saved filter 40865 (project = ES AND status != Closed AND component IN (Payroll, "Reports - Payroll")), verified directly against Jira's filter API when this section was built — so this list can't quietly drift from what that saved filter shows.
  • No separate data pull — reads the shared Jira store, which picks up every ES change within about 5 minutes.
  • Each ES ticket is cross-referenced against Zendesk's real structured "ES Number" field (not a subject-text guess) on every currently open ticket tagged jira_escalated where either its CORE-Module is Payroll or its group is Payroll Specialist — broader than just this poll's own Payroll Specialist group scope, since a ticket can be escalated while sitting with a different team (e.g. Email Team) but still be a Payroll issue by module. A match shows "Linked from Zendesk #…" with that ticket's current status; no match means the ES ticket isn't currently linked from any open ticket meeting either criterion (e.g. the originating ticket has since been solved).
  • Its own independent population — not a slice of the open-ticket counts/charts above, and not affected by the GAL/Gusto exclusion rules (an ES ticket can be linked to a ticket that's also sitting in the GAL section, since GAL and ES references are independent tags on the same subject line).

What counts every open ticket vs. the working set

  • The "Open tickets" KPI and the top scope line are the only two numbers on this page that count every open ticket, GAL and Gusto Incidents included. Every other count, chart, and the main table work off the subset excluding both. The "Created Off-Hours" KPI and its chart are the one exception — they intentionally cover every open ticket, GAL/Gusto included, since arrival time is a separate question from who's handling the reply.

Created off-hours / weekend

  • Based on the ticket's creation timestamp converted to Pacific and compared against Payroll's real coverage window (Mon-Fri 6am-4pm Pacific — see [[payroll_business_hours]] in the team's own records; this is NOT the same as Zendesk's built-in business-hours schedules, which don't match). Three buckets: Business hours, After-hours (weekday outside 6am-4pm), and Weekend.
  • This only covers tickets that are currently still open — it's a snapshot of today's open backlog by arrival time, not a full historical rate across every Payroll ticket ever created (solved/closed tickets aren't in this poll's population at all). Read it as "of what's open right now, how much came in outside coverage hours," not as an all-time volume metric.
  • Tracked in the trend chart above (the "Off-hours/weekend" line). Counts saved before this board went live don't have it.

Exporting an agent's list

  • "Copy list" copies a plain-text ticket list (id, link, subject, hours, flags) to your clipboard for that agent — paste it into an email, Slack message, or Teams chat.

Reviewed checkbox & notes

  • Every ticket — in the table and in every drilldown, including the ES Escalations (Payroll) section — has a Reviewed checkbox and a free-text note field, so you can mark what you've already looked at and leave a note without leaving the page.
  • These are shared: everyone who can open this board sees the same checkboxes and notes, with who changed them last and when. They stay on this board only; nothing is written to Zendesk or Jira.
  • Recheck: once a ticket has been marked Reviewed on a prior calendar day, an extra "Reviewed <date> — recheck & leave a fresh note?" checkbox appears next to its note. Checking it archives that note into a small "N earlier notes" history log (so the context isn't lost) and resets the ticket back to unreviewed with a blank note, ready for today's pass.

Live queue and calls

  • The queue panel shows the RingCX Payroll Specialist queue from the snapshot RingCX's wallboard data gives every minute: callers waiting, the longest wait (flashes red at 5 minutes), agents available, and today's presented, answered and abandoned calls. RingCX counts SLA as answered within 30 seconds.
  • "Agents on Payroll" lists everyone assigned to the Payroll queue in RingCX, plus anyone on a Payroll call right now. Colors follow RingCX's base states: green Available, red on a call, yellow Working-type statuses, grey breaks, lunch and logged out.
  • The call lists come from RingCX's call report, refreshed every 15 minutes during Payroll hours. "Missed" means the caller hung up while waiting or while an agent's phone was ringing.
  • Direct vs. transferred: RingCX's report keeps one row per call, filed under the last queue but naming the first agent. A call answered on CORE and transferred to Payroll therefore names the CORE agent. So a call answered by someone on the Payroll queue counts as Direct; one first answered by anyone else counts as Transferred in, and "Transferred in by" shows who sent it. "Who answered on Payroll" credits the Payroll agent on a transfer when the Zendesk call tickets (one per agent leg) show one; these exist from 10/14 on, so older transfers often show "Payroll leg not found". Median answer time uses direct calls only.

Recently checked in (internal note)

  • If the most recent comment on a ticket — of any kind, public or a private/internal note — is an internal note posted in Zendesk within the last 24 hours, this page shows "✓ Recently checked in" with the note text.
  • A recently-checked-in ticket drops out of every flagged/needs-attention view (KPIs, urgency tier, by-assignee, by-reason, Where to Focus) for as long as the note stands — it's still an open ticket (shows in the main table, status chart, and GAL/Gusto sections with the badge) — but as soon as anything newer happens (a customer reply, a public reply) it reappears as needing attention.

Refresh

  • The page refreshes itself every minute while it's open. Ticket changes show up within a few minutes of happening in Zendesk; the queue is at most about a minute old.
  • The trend chart gets one point per hour on weekdays, plus the history kept by the earlier Watchlist since 8/26.
